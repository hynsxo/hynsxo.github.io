Robotics · Embedded SystemsDec 2024 – Feb 2025
Undergraduate Intern, AI Research Group, Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST)
10-DOF Dual-Arm Drumming Robot
C/C++CANopenGDB

I developed a 10-DOF dual-arm robot that reads a score and plays the drums. At every strike, the arm motion was slightly off.

  • Traced the CANopen data and found that at a control-mode switch, motors used the position from the previous 5 ms cycle instead of the latest one.
  • Ruled out shortening the cycle to 1 ms, since the whole IK-based motion pipeline was built around 5 ms.
  • Kept the existing cyclic PDO communication and added SDO reads of the latest position only at mode switches (hybrid design).
  • After a sim-to-real collision broke a part, introduced a three-step check for new motions: simulation → 50% speed on the real robot → log errors and add trajectory margins.
  • Also: Dijkstra and interpolation for trajectories, PID tuning, GDB debugging, collaboration and code-flow docs.

Made the striking motion smooth with minimal change to the existing control structure. The three-step check led to zero collisions and became the team standard.

Data AnalyticsApr 2024 – Aug 2024
Land & Transportation Data Utilization Competition
Team UAIN · Korea Airports Corporation President’s Award (Excellence Prize)
PythonOpenCVQGISRegression Analysis

There was no way to check residential noise impact when designing urban air mobility (UAM) routes over the city. Our two-person team built a noise-analysis model; I handled the area noise-sensitivity weights.

  • Chose three sites from metropolitan transport-demand data: Gangnam, Hongik Univ. Station, Seoul Station.
  • Mapped residential and commercial density in QGIS and set area weights from the noise-limit ratio in Korea’s Noise and Vibration Control Act.
  • My teammate built distance-based noise regression models from Gimpo Airport measurements and UAM certification limits.
  • When our results didn’t fit, we compared both models side by side as diagrams; mine was missing altitude, so I split the weights by altitude.
  • Compared predicted noise and weights on a 17-cell grid along each of four route directions.

Showed in numbers that results differ by route direction at the same site (Seoul Station, above-average noise area: 39.7% east–west vs. 31.8% southwest–northeast), winning the Korea Airports Corporation President’s Award.
