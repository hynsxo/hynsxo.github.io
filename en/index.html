About

Most of what I've worked on comes down to one thing: making sure data arrives on time, with the right value. When a robot joint receives its position one cycle late, the drum beat smears. When one digit of an overseas account number is wrong, the customer pays a cancellation fee. When two vehicles at an intersection learn each other's positions too late, they collide.

The domain changed every time, but what I had to check never did: where is this data coming from, where is it going, and how accurately is it getting there? CAN communication in a robot, noise data along a UAM flight path, an overseas-remittance pipeline at a bank, V2V communication between vehicles — the same question, wearing different clothes.

When familiar coursework wasn't enough, I went back to page one of a communication protocol document, or took a course outside my major. What follows is a record of the choices I made along the way — and what difference each one made, and for whom.

🤖 Robotics / EmbeddedDec 2024 – Feb 2025
Undergraduate Field Intern, AI Research Center
Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST) — 10-DOF Dual-Arm Drum-Playing Robot
C/C++CAN (CANopen)GDB

A 10-DOF dual-arm robot was meant to play the drums, but the arms drifted slightly out of sync on every strike and the sound smeared. The team approached it by adjusting the motor control cycle, without improvement. Tracing the communication data myself, I found that motor position values were arriving later than the existing 5 ms cycle at the exact moment the control mode switched.

  • Confirmed that motor position-value synchronization lagged every time the system switched between position and torque control modes, and judged that the existing PDO-only scheme couldn't deliver the needed real-time performance.
  • Proposed and implemented a hybrid PDO+SDO communication scheme to read real-time motor position values directly.
  • Built a trajectory-generation algorithm (Dijkstra + interpolation) to minimize unnecessary torso movement, tuned motor PID parameters, and debugged system-level issues with GDB.
  • Set up a GitHub-convention-based version-control workflow to support future contributors.

The strike motion became natural and the overall timeline shrank by about two months. What stayed with me longer, though, is that going back to page one of the motor protocol document turned out to be the fastest route once the familiar approach stopped working. I left behind a performance-state diagram of the whole code flow so the next person wouldn't have to retrace it.

🧠 AI / BackendMar – Jun 2026
FollowMe — Personalized Prediction Service
Graduation Capstone, Team Project — Server & RAG Pipeline Design
FastAPIAWS EC2FAISSSentence TransformersGPT API

I've kept a diary every day for eight years. Even so, I'd plan tomorrow without accounting for what might actually happen that day, and often failed to follow through. The project started from that inconvenience of my own. I owned the server, the RAG pipeline, and the next-day prediction and mission-suggestion logic.

  • Separated responsibilities so the app handles only input storage and display, while a FastAPI server on AWS EC2 takes on embedding, vector search, and GPT calls — letting app and server scale and be maintained independently.
  • Chose diary text over structured personality scores as the retrieval target, since a person's recurring patterns surfaced in sentences carrying emotion and context rather than in numbers.
  • Prediction runs at midnight: the diary just written becomes the query, FAISS retrieves the nearest past entries by L2 distance, and those are injected into the GPT prompt as grounding.
  • Selected paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 (384-dim, lightweight) against three constraints — Korean text, running on a GPU-less EC2 instance, sentence-similarity search — and deployed it to the server myself.
  • Masked sensitive fields such as phone and national ID numbers with regex before storage, and built a self-correction loop where completing a mission immediately updates the personality score feeding the next prediction.

Thirty classmates used it for a month. Watching the retrieved past entries and the reasoning behind each prediction diverge from user to user as records accumulated, I felt for the first time that a structure I'd designed was actually reaching someone's day.

📊 Data AnalysisApr – Aug 2024
National Land & Transportation Data Competition
Team UAIN — Korea Airports Corporation President's Award (Excellence Award)
PythonOpenCVQGISRegression

More than 200 UAM routes are projected to be in service after 2035, but at the time there was no clear route-design guideline beyond the fixed-corridor demonstrations. Aviation noise carries across a wide area, so more routes means more noise reaching residential neighborhoods. We proposed an integrated noise analysis model that computes noise impact at the route-design stage, before anything flies.

  • Used metropolitan transit demand data to select three points in central Seoul where UAM demand would concentrate (Gangnam, Hongdae, Seoul Station), excluding Jamsil where Lotte World Tower introduces too many route-design variables.
  • Processed Ministry of Land building-use data in QGIS to map residential-versus-commercial ratios per site, then derived district-level noise-sensitivity weights from the residential and commercial thresholds in the Noise and Vibration Control Act.
  • Built a distance-based noise prediction model by integrating a polynomial regression on measured Gimpo Airport noise data with a log-scale regression grounded in UAM noise certification standards.
  • Set four route orientations (N–S, E–W, SW–NE, SE–NW) through each vertiport and applied predicted noise values and district weights across a grid extending 8 cells either side of the route (50 m per cell, 19 cells total) to analyze impact cell by cell.

We could show numerically that noise impact differs by route orientation even at the same site — at Seoul Station, the share of cells above the average noise weight ranged from 39.7% (E–W) down to 31.8% (SW–NE). We proposed the model as a basis for UAM route-design guidelines, operators' own route planning, and vertiport noise-mitigation zoning, and it earned the Korea Airports Corporation President's Award.

A semester spent taking one-stage detection from theory to a trained model to an actual function — air-conditioning control. I spent longer deciding what to do with the model's output than building the model itself.
