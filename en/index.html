Robotics · Embedded SystemsDec 2024 – Feb 2025
Undergraduate Intern, AI Research Group, Korea Institute of Science and Technology (KIST)
10-DOF Dual-Arm Drumming Robot
C/C++CANopenGDB

I worked on drumming motions for a 10-DOF dual-arm robot. The arms were slightly out of sync at the moment of impact, and adjusting the control cycle did not resolve the issue. I traced CANopen communication data and the motor-control flow to identify the cause.

  • Identified delays in motor-position updates beyond the 5 ms cycle during position-to-torque control-mode transitions.
  • Proposed and implemented a hybrid communication design that supplemented PDO messaging with SDO reads to check position values during mode transitions.
  • Applied Dijkstra’s algorithm and interpolation to generate trajectories that reduced torso movement; tuned motor PID parameters and debugged with GDB.
  • Investigated a real-world collision that did not appear in simulation and found that joint-component wear was not represented in the simulation model.
  • Documented GitHub collaboration conventions and created a performance-state-based code-flow diagram for the team.

The communication changes improved strike timing and shortened the overall development schedule by about two months. The project also taught me to validate simulation results against real hardware, and I documented the code and system structure to make follow-up development easier.

Data AnalyticsApr 2024 – Aug 2024
Land & Transportation Data Utilization Competition
Team UAIN · Korea Airports Corporation President’s Award (Excellence Prize)
PythonOpenCVQGISRegression Analysis

I proposed a noise-analysis model that combines measured aircraft-noise data with building-use information, allowing the potential noise impact on surrounding areas to be compared during UAM route planning.

  • Selected Gangnam, Hongik University Station, and Seoul Station as analysis sites based on metropolitan transport-demand data indicating likely UAM demand.
  • Mapped building-use proportions in QGIS and calculated area-sensitivity weights using noise standards for residential and commercial zones.
  • Combined a polynomial regression model based on measured noise at Gimpo Airport with a log-scale regression model based on UAM noise-certification criteria to estimate noise by distance.
  • Compared four route orientations and grid-level predictions to analyze how route direction affects noise exposure.

At Seoul Station, the share of areas with above-average weighted noise was 39.7% for the east–west route and 31.8% for the southwest–northeast route. I helped propose the analysis as a reference for route planning and noise-mitigation area reviews; the team received the Korea Airports Corporation President’s Award.

AI · BackendMar 2026 – Jun 2026
FollowMe — Diary-Based Personalized Forecasting Service
Capstone Team Project · Backend and RAG Design
FastAPIAWS EC2FAISSMiniLMGPT API

The idea grew from eight years of journaling and noticing how difficult it is to follow plans that ignore one’s condition and circumstances. I designed the backend and RAG architecture for a service that retrieves past entries to forecast the next day and suggest personalized missions.

  • Separated responsibilities so the app handled input and display, while a FastAPI server on AWS EC2 handled embeddings, vector search, and GPT API calls.
  • Used diary text rather than personality scores as the search corpus, drawing on entries that captured past emotions and circumstances as evidence for predictions.
  • Used the day’s diary entry as a query for L2-distance search in FAISS, added similar past entries to the GPT prompt, and ran the forecast at midnight.
  • Selected and deployed paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 (384 dimensions) based on Korean-language support, sentence-similarity performance, and CPU-only execution on EC2.
  • Masked sensitive information such as phone numbers and resident registration numbers using regular expressions, and implemented a feedback loop that updated personality scores based on mission completion for use in later forecasts.

Thirty students from the same course used the service for one month. In real use, I observed how retrieved examples and prediction evidence differed according to each user’s history.
